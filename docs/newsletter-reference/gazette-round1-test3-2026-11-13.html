Bracket Madness Around the Country

Round 1, Week 3 finals: the top eight series in every region. Each series is best of three.

It Ain't Over Till It's Over

There are two tournaments going on. The bracket crowns one champion: lose a best-of-three series and a school is out of it. But nobody goes home.

Every knocked-out school keeps playing a game every week, and every run its alumni score keeps counting. The boards below rank each region's schools by total runs. After Round 10 (Week 30), the runs leader in each of the eight regions moves on, for the right to play the eventual 1,024-team bracket champion.

Knocked-out schools are paired across regions, and real-life teammates come first: when two schools' alumni play on the same team, their schools face each other.

THE BUSINESS OF HOMETOWN BASEBALL

Businesses don't just want to advertise to a community anymore. They want to belong to one.

Sponsorship used to be simple: buy signage, put up a logo, count impressions. Now brands want something deeper. Carvana just became Arizona Athletics' first basketball jersey-patch sponsor, in a deal that even includes Carvana-sponsored Wildcats alumni reunions. Broadview Federal Credit Union put its patch on all 19 of UAlbany's Division I teams, a partnership the school called a commitment to its Capital Region.

The same idea works closer to home. A local restaurant, bank, insurance agency or auto dealer doesn't need millions of national impressions. It wants to be part of the few thousand people it actually serves, and Hamilton's YAT?STATS hub is built for exactly that: businesses backing the continuing stories of local players and alumni.

"The real value isn't putting another logo next to a box score. It's connecting a business to a community it actually cares about."

PETER DELUCA, FOUNDER, YAT?STATS

FROM THE ARCHIVES · ON YOUTUBE

Remember when the King of JUCO tried out for Hamilton?

Last November, YouTuber Eric Sim, the King of JUCO, spent a day at an off-season Hamilton practice to find out if a 36-year-old could make one of the top high school programs in Arizona.

Coach Mike Woods put him through the same paces as the Huskies: the velo machine in the cage, live hitting off a double machine, a bunt station and live at-bats against Hamilton's pitchers, with TrackMan calling a big-league strike zone. Kris DeSantiago, now at Michigan, was his partner for the day.

"You're just going to have to earn it."

MIKE WOODS, BEFORE THE TRYOUT

He hit some tanks, got carved up by breaking balls, and at the end of the day Woods told him he made the team, once they work out "the birth certificate stuff."
